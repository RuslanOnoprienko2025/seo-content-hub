## The Problems That Show Up Later

Even if you go through the process, the real issues usually show up later. This is where many HTML-based signature tools fall short.

### Unreliable Image Hosting

A large number of signatures generated by free tools rely on externally hosted images: logos, icons, or banners.

Some providers host images on their own servers, and those URLs can change. There have been cases where users were required to regenerate signatures after providers updated their image infrastructure, breaking previously created signatures.

When that happens, images simply stop loading. The signature remains in place, but key visual elements disappear.

### Inconsistent Rendering Across Email Clients

Email clients don't follow a single standard for HTML rendering.

A signature that looks perfectly aligned in Gmail may shift in Outlook. Apple Mail may handle spacing differently. Mobile clients often simplify or strip styling altogether.

This means that even the best free email signature generator can only guarantee how the signature looks in its own preview, not how it behaves across environments. Most free tools don't test across all major clients. They optimize for visual output, not cross-platform reliability.

### Lack of Maintenance and Updates

Because these tools are secondary features, support is limited. They don't receive consistent updates or long-term support. Bugs persist, compatibility issues remain unresolved, and changes in email client behavior aren't addressed quickly.

Over time, this reduces reliability, even if the tool worked initially.

## Why Templates Don't Solve the Problem

Many people browse through the best free email signature templates looking for something ready-made.

But templates solve only one part of the problem: layout.

They don't guarantee that the signature will:
- render correctly in all email clients
- survive forwarding and reply chains
- display consistently on mobile devices

In practice, a simple HTML signature built with text-based elements is more reliable than image-heavy templates. Clean HTML code with text formatting stays consistent and avoids most rendering issues that come from embedded images or complex styling.
